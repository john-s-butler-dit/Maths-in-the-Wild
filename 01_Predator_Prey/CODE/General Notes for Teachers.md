## General Notes for Teachers

In this module students will learn how programming can be used to model our environment using the example of the predator prey relationship.
The module is divided into eight lessons with an extra bonus on how to model diseases in the population.



Lessons:

__Lesson 1__

In this lesson students will load real world data from the Hudson Bay Company (1865-1935) on the number of pelts they collected from Lynxes and Snow Hares. The students will then plot the data in different ways and comment on what patterns they observe.

__Lesson 2__
In this lesson students will simulate exponential growth of Snow Hares in the wild with no predators using a for loop. This will introduce the concept of recurrence and birth (growth) rate. 

__Lesson 3__
In this lesson students will simulate exponential decline of Lynxes in the wild with no prey using a for loop. This will reinforce the concept of recurrence and death (growth) rate. It mirrors the previous lesson reinforcing some of methods.


__Lesson 4__
In this lesson students will combine their previous work and add the interaction of the animals. This will show how by adding a simple operation complexity can arise. The lesson includes for loops, plotting and interpretation of data.
__Lesson 5__
The final lesson links back to the re-introduction of Wolves to Yellowstone Park. This is a brainstorming lesson about how they might model these interactions. It will also include a proposed solution to situation.
